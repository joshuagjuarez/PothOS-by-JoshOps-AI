# Continuing ArcheAgents: history, corpus and daily work

Updated November 7, 2026.

**Status: product requirements and public qualification criteria.** This document
describes the continuity a released PothOS configuration must demonstrate. It does
not install a runtime, instantiate an ArcheAgent or establish that a background
service is currently running. See [release status](RELEASE_STATUS.md).

An ArcheAgent should carry a coherent history and body of permitted source across
conversations, projects, devices and engine changes. Completing one engineering
request is only one event in that history. Personal projects, learning, creative
work, decisions, corrections, relationships and unresolved obligations also need
continuity within the permissions chosen by the person responsible for them.

## A profile with an attributable past

The existing identity and memory owners should maintain these connected views:

| View | Required contents |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and charter | Stable identity, chosen name, purpose, responsibilities, governing source and accepted amendments |
| Profile | User-approved interaction preferences, capabilities, limitations and current assignments, separately from the engine or device serving a session |
| History | Source-linked interactions, appointments, decisions, corrections, work and returns, with their original times and recorded standing |
| Corpus | Permitted originals and the references needed to retrieve them, including corrections, provenance, access scope and known omissions |
| Current obligations | Existing work, dependencies, owners, status, evidence, remaining action and return destination |

A biographical account is a view of that recorded history. It must distinguish
observed events, a person's original words, interpretations, proposals and
accepted corrections. It must not invent experiences or turn an uncertain
inference into a fact. Agent history and a person's private life remain separately
attributed, even when the person chooses to connect them.

The profile is not an access grant. A name, role, hierarchy position, personality
label or model choice cannot authorize a tool or disclose another person's
information. People must be able to inspect and correct retained material under
the applicable custody and recovery rules. Public examples use synthetic data;
private identities, voices and corpora are not part of the public distribution.

## Daily continuity uses the original work

The daily view should recover the existing work register before proposing new
work. A new day or a new session does not create a new copy of every obligation.

1. Recover the original objective, source, accepted amendments, existing work and
   attempt identifiers, and actual latest owner return.
2. Preserve issued parent/child ancestry, dependency links and any existing leaf
   or cut references. Their meanings come from the original work owner; an
   adapter must not reinterpret a historical cut as permission to replay or close
   work.
3. Reconcile prior results at their recorded standing. Recover uncertain attempts
   by their original identity. Reuse applicable evidence without repeating its
   originating effect. Keep completed work in history rather than reissuing it.
4. Present the next eligible action with its owner, dependencies, limits and return
   obligation. Keep held or disputed items visible with the reason and evidence.
5. Record what changed and what remains. Preparation, delivery, acknowledgment,
   acceptance, running, verification and closure are distinct states.

A daily workflow card is a recoverable view of this existing work and its current
review. It must link to the full source rather than substitute a new summary for
the original record. A missing day is a coverage gap to reconcile, not an event
to fabricate or an instruction to repeat every past effect.

## Metacognitive review is runtime behavior

Here, metacognition means observable review of the system's own context,
assumptions, plan, execution and result. Useful behavior includes recognizing
missing source, checking contradictions, retaining dissent, comparing an outcome
with its acceptance criteria, and correcting the next step when evidence changes.
Interaction preferences can guide how these checks are communicated; a
personality type does not establish their implementation or effectiveness.

Use the existing work, memory, admission, verification and service owners for
these checks. They belong at supported ingress, source changes, work admission,
before consequential effects, on result return, and during recovery. Recurring
review should inspect pending obligations and relevant runtime health evidence, then
select only eligible work within its current authority and resource limits.

An always-on claim requires an actual enabled service or scheduler, an accountable
owner, observable last-run and next-due state, bounded resource use, original-ID
recovery, and a demonstrated restart path. Event hooks and a daily schedule are
different mechanisms and must be identified separately. If the service is paused,
unreachable or missing, the interface should show that state and the outstanding
review. Written instructions alone do not run a recurring process.

## Return, recovery and release evidence

The same work and permitted history must remain recoverable after a supported
change of chat, session, engine or device. Each receiver must be authorized for
the selected sources. A returned result retains its original identity and
evidence; current disclosure rules still apply. A child's success does not close
its parent, and saved source does not by itself prove recipient acceptance.

A configuration should demonstrate at least these synthetic tests before claiming
the corresponding behavior:

- Create an operator-owned identity and profile, record a sourced event and a
  correction, restart, and recover both with their original provenance.
- Change a supported execution engine while retaining the same identity and
  admitted work, without silently selecting another account or provider.
- Recover a mixture of open, completed, held and uncertain legacy work into a
  daily view without duplicate execution or invented closure.
- Run the registered review trigger, observe its result and next-due state, stop
  and restart it, and recover the same work after an interrupted attempt.
- Refuse unauthorized corpus reads and returns, including after permissions
  change, while preserving separately authorized work.
- Read back the exact saved record through an authorized receiver and recover
  the original result without replaying its effect.

Publish the exact source/build, configuration, observation time, results and
remaining limits for each qualified path. A profile document, successful prompt,
source test or familiar conversational voice alone cannot prove the full loop.
